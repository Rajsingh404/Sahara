### Held out by recording session

The cross-validation above deals clips into folds at random, so other clips from the same sitting (same phone, room and often the same doorbell or appliance) are in training. Here whole sessions are held out (`scripts/organise_recordings.py: session_group`: the named recorder, or the file-name series of unattributed uploads), so every clip is scored by a model that never heard that phone or place (`models/logs/20261030-185320_a_real_s3/team_cv_session.json`). ## Name calls and caller ID

A separate model, not YAMNet: pretrained speech embeddings (openWakeWord) with a small "Aryan" head spot the name for any voice; a pretrained speaker encoder (GE2E) then names an enrolled caller, or says "Someone". Leave-one-recorder-out on the team recordings (`docs/name_call_eval.md`, regenerated on this machine by `scripts/eval_name_call.py`; design in `docs/name_call.md`).

|---|---:|---:|
| Background scenes (TV, kitchen, street...) | 0.872 | 19% |
| Similar names (Arya, Aryanshi, other names) | 0.743 | 32% |

At the equal-error threshold (0.62): 79% of calls caught, 15% of background pieces and 36% of similar names raise a false call.

## Known limitations

- Window-level metrics count 0.5 s windows, so long clips weigh more than short ones; clip detection is the closer proxy for what a user experiences.
- The Indian team test set is small (a few clips per class) and split per label, so the same phone or room can appear in train and test. Smoke alarm, siren and glass break have 8 Indian test clips each.
- The team's third upload (2026-10-30: 161 clips, the first team smoke alarms, sirens and glass breaks) led to this model. Validation is public audio only, so it cannot see what the new recordings add: the previous model (`20261005-195620_b_synthetic`, never trained on them) still scores higher there (0.643 against 0.634). Selection is therefore among runs trained on the current data. On the same test set the previous model reaches public mAP 0.789 and Indian mAP 0.698 and alerts on 31 of 105 Indian clips (knocks 2/27, glass 0/8), at 3.49 false alarms/h.
- Tuning the alert thresholds on Indian audio was tried and not kept (2026-10-30, run `20261030-024710_b_synthetic`, `organise_recordings.py --val-fraction 0.2`): 20 % of each team label moved to validation, Indian clips counted for half of each class's detection. On the unchanged test set it alerted on 15 of 41 Indian clips (18 now), 1 of 11 Indian knocks (2 now) and 21 of 27 smoke alarms (23 now), at 3.2 false alarms/h. Six Indian knocks in validation are too few to set a threshold; more knock recordings are the fix. (Measured before the third upload added 40 knocks.)
- knocking, appliance_beep and doorbell have the lowest test AP; their detection is limited by the model, not the alert settings (see the budget trade-off above).
- Synthetic results are reported separately and never as real-world performance.
- Safety-critical classes (smoke_alarm, siren, glass_break) carry a 3x positive weight in the loss and a precision floor in model selection; how many windows each needs is tuned like every other class (see Alert settings), and their false alarms are listed above.
